Our approach

Merging the historical record with modern technology

Water management is shifting from periodic monitoring toward continuous data and models. Both still matter. Decades of field sampling and gauge records are the ground truth any credible model has to answer to, and modern sensors and satellites see what a monthly site visit never could.

OpenCurrent brings the two together. We bring weather-style forecasting to water quality and quantity by anchoring modern machine learning in historical ground truth, so the record you already have makes new data more useful, and new data makes the record more valuable.

We are candid about the methods we use, where they fall short, and how much confidence to place in each estimate. And we build with our partners, so the teams that rely on a forecast can understand, run, and own the code behind it.

Current work

What we are building now

Fire-to-faucet forecasting for Denver Water

As water-system lead in the NSF ASCEND Engine's ARID program, we are assembling a public benchmark dataset that links wildfire, remote sensing, hydrology, and water-quality records across the western US, training models to forecast post-fire turbidity, organic carbon, and algal conditions, and building a prototype decision-support tool for Denver Water's supply network.

Hourly bacteria nowcasts from in-stream sensors

For a sensor manufacturer, we run an hourly nowcast feed that combines optical sensor readings with lab-confirmed samples at dozens of recreational-water sites in North America and Europe. Every prediction is scored against the lab result once it arrives, and each site is served by whichever method has the best track record there.
